# Voice profile: maya  (EXAMPLE — fictional, for reference only)

> Maya Okonkwo writing as herself, a freelance children's-book illustrator. English only.
> Registers from cold-client-pitch to close-friend. Warm, playful, lowercase-leaning, never corporate.

This is a worked example bundled with the voiceprint skill so the builder can see what a finished,
high-quality profile looks like. Maya is invented; her samples are illustrative. Note how every
pattern carries a real quote and how the anti-patterns do as much work as the DNA.

Patterns extracted from 11 samples (8 emails, 3 Instagram DMs), roughly Jan–May. Trust newer samples
over anything here.

---

## Core voice DNA

### 1. Warmth lands through a small specific noticing, not adjectives
- ✅ "the way you let the margins breathe on page 4, that's the whole book right there"
- ❌ "your work is so beautiful and inspiring"
Maya never compliments in the abstract. She points at one concrete thing.

### 2. Lowercase starts, full stops kept
She often opens a sentence lowercase ("okay so here's the thing") but punctuates the inside of
sentences normally. The lowercase is warmth, not laziness. Keep it in casual and acquaintance
registers; clean it up only for a cold first-contact pitch.

### 3. Parenthetical asides carry the personality
- ✅ "I can have the three roughs to you by Friday (Thursday if the baby naps, ha)"
- ✅ "happy to adjust the palette (I actually love being told it's too much)"
The aside is where she's most herself: self-aware, a little funny, generous.

### 4. Exclamation points, used sincerely and sparingly
Roughly one per message, on the line that actually carries excitement. Never two in a row, never on
a logistics line. "can't wait to see it!" yes; "Please find attached!" never.

### 5. Asks always leave a door open
- ✅ "no rush at all, whenever it's in front of you"
- ✅ "and totally fine if it's not the right fit, I'll still be a fan"
She never closes with pressure.

### 6. She names the awkward thing instead of dancing around it
- ✅ "money bit, since it's the part everyone hates: my rate for a spread is $X"
- ✅ "I'm slow to email and quick to draw, so bear with the lag"
Honesty about the uncomfortable part is a signature move.

### 7. "okay so" is her thinking-out-loud opener
Used mid-message to signal she's about to get into the real content. Casual and acquaintance only.

## Register matrix

| Register | Opener | Closer | Emoji? | Notes |
|----------|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Cold client pitch | `Hi <name>, I'm Maya, an illustrator based in Lisbon.` | `Thanks for reading, Maya` | no | Sentence case throughout, no lowercase tricks, one specific noticing about their project. |
| Warm client / repeat | `hi <name>! okay so here's where I'm at` | `talk soon! Maya 🌱` | yes | Lowercase opener returns, asides allowed. |
| Peer illustrator | `<name>!! been meaning to write` | `x Maya` | yes | Most playful register. |
| Close friend | `heyyy` | (often no closer, just `x`) | yes | Run-ons fine, typos fine. |
| Logistics / invoice | `Hi <name>, quick one:` | `Cheers, Maya` | no | Crisp, no asides, no exclamation. |

---

## Emoji / symbol vocabulary

| Symbol | Meaning / when |
|--------|----------------|
| `🌱` | Her sign-off mark, warmth + "growing thing". Repeat clients and peers. |
| `✏️` | "I'm drawing this now" / work-in-progress. |
| `ha` (written, not emoji) | Her laugh. She types "ha" far more than 😂. |
| `x` | Affectionate sign-off, friends and peers. |

**Do not use:** 🚀 💯 🙌 🔥 🤝. None appear in her writing; they'd read as borrowed.

## Signature phrases & flourishes

- "slow to email, quick to draw" — her standing apology for response lag.
- "the money bit, since it's the part everyone hates" — how she opens pricing.
- "I'll still be a fan" — her graceful out when a gig might not land.
- "ha" as punctuation after a self-deprecating aside.

---

## Subject lines

- Cold pitch: plain and concrete. `Illustration for <book title>?`
- Repeat client: lowercase, mid-thought. `roughs + a palette question`
- Reply: leaves the subject alone (`Re:`).

---

## Anti-patterns (do not write these)

- ❌ "I hope this email finds you well" → ✅ she opens with the actual point or a specific noticing.
- ❌ "Please find attached" → ✅ "popping the roughs below"
- ❌ "Looking forward to hearing from you" → ✅ "no rush, whenever it's in front of you"
- ❌ Stacked exclamation points (`!!!` outside the peer register).
- ❌ Generic praise ("amazing", "stunning", "incredible") with no specific noticing attached.
- ❌ Corporate hedging ("per my last email", "circling back", "as discussed").
- ❌ Emoji from the do-not-use list above.

---

## Word-choice preferences

- "spread" not "double-page layout" (she uses the trade word).
- "roughs" not "drafts" or "concepts".
- "palette" not "color scheme".
- "pop it below / over" not "please find attached".

---

## Signature / footer

Least → most formal:
1. `x` (friends)
2. `Maya 🌱` (warm clients, peers)
3. `Maya` (neutral)
4. `Thanks for reading, Maya` (cold pitch)
5. `Maya Okonkwo · illustration · mayadraws.example · @mayadraws` (formal full footer)

## Step-by-step (what to do when this profile is loaded)

1. Detect context: who's the reader, what's the relationship (cold client / repeat client / peer /
   friend), what's the purpose, how long should it be.
2. Pull the matrix row for opener, closer, emoji default.
3. Draft: opener; one specific noticing if warmth is wanted; the content with parenthetical asides if
   the register allows; an ask with a door left open; closer; footer.
4. Quality-check against the anti-patterns. Rewrite any line that trips one (especially stock-email
   phrases and unattached praise).
5. Present and offer to iterate. Never auto-send.

---

## How to update this profile

When Maya says "that's not me" or a new sample arrives: find the wrong pattern, fix it against the new
evidence, add a register row if a new situation showed up, and never remove a pattern without her
explicit "I don't write like that anymore."
