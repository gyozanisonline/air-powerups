# Finding the right samples

Voice lives in writing a person has already done. The hard part of Build mode is rarely the
analysis, it is that people do not know what counts as a good sample or where to look. This guide
helps them find their best raw material fast, and helps you know what to skip.

Use it during **Build, Step 1**. Offer it proactively, because most people underestimate how much of
their own voice is sitting in their sent folder right now.

## The one rule for what counts

A sample is useful only if it is **genuinely written by the person** and **not edited by an AI**.
Everything else is a question of range: the richest profile comes from samples that span the
situations they actually write in, from a cold stranger to their closest friend.

## Where voice lives, ranked by signal

### 1. Sent email — the richest source

A person's sent folder is the single best corpus of how they write: high volume, and it naturally
spans registers (clients, colleagues, friends, bureaucracy) in their real, unguarded voice. Start
here.

- **If an email or Gmail connection is available** in this environment, offer it directly: *"Want me
  to learn your voice from your sent mail? I'll read a range of your sent emails, not just the most
  recent, and skim out anything that looks AI-polished."* Pull a spread across recipients and tones,
  not twenty near-identical work emails.
- **If no connection exists**, suggest the easy manual paths:
  - Gmail: search `in:sent`, open a dozen varied messages, paste them.
  - Any mail client: forward a handful of varied sent messages, or export them.
  - Bulk: an export (e.g. Google Takeout) can hand you the whole sent folder as a file to read.
- Keep it a soft, opt-in suggestion, never automatic. Reading someone's mail is theirs to allow.

### 2. Personal chat and DMs — the casual register

WhatsApp, iMessage, Slack or Discord DMs, Instagram DMs. This is where the most casual, intimate
register lives, the one formal writing never shows. Most apps have an "export chat" option
(WhatsApp: chat menu, then Export chat, without media). Great for capturing how they actually talk
to people they are close to.

### 3. Long-form they authored

Blog posts, newsletters, essays, social posts, substantial docs or READMEs they wrote. Good for
their "thinking out loud" voice. One caution: published long-form is often edited, sometimes
heavily, so it shows their polished register, not their natural one. Use it alongside rawer sources,
not instead of them.

### 4. Public-but-casual writing

Reviews, comments, forum or Reddit posts, issue threads. Lower volume, but often candid and
opinionated, which surfaces strong voice markers.

## What to skip (it poisons the profile)

- **Anything AI-written or AI-polished.** This is the big one. Extract from a model-smoothed draft
  and you capture the model's voice, not theirs, which is the exact failure this skill exists to
  prevent. When in doubt, ask for a rougher version.
- **Forwarded or quoted content** they did not write.
- **Dead transactional lines** ("ok thanks", "sounds good") with no voice in them.
- **Collaborative docs** where you cannot tell which words are theirs.
- **One register only**, if they want a versatile voice. Ten formal newsletters teach you
  formal-only. Say so out loud and ask for a casual sample to balance it.

## How to use what you gather

Aim for the 5 to 15 varied samples Step 1 asks for. If a connection let you pull a large batch (a
whole sent folder, say), do not feed all of it in blindly: select a spread across recipients and
tones, drop the AI-polished and the purely transactional, and keep the ones where personality
shows. Quality and range beat raw volume.
